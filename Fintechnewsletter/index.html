Today's News

Jeonbuk Bank launches South Korea's first Bitcoin-backed won loans

Jeonbuk Bank is the first bank in South Korea to offer won-denominated, Bitcoin-backed loans to corporate clients, designed to fund business operations. A registered custodian, not the bank, holds the Bitcoin. Collateral is valued using prices from multiple global exchanges, and a default triggers a court-ordered sale under rules effective 1 October.

Source: Future of Banking, 13 Oct 2026

Revolut says it has built proprietary AI models on customer transaction data

CEO Nik Storonsky said Revolut has developed proprietary AI models trained on customer transaction data. The newsletter says Revolut processes 30 to 40 million transactions daily and plans to use its data capabilities to build a broader range of technology products beyond financial services.

Source: Future of Banking, 13 Oct 2026

Standard Bank to take up to 4.99% of OPay via a proposed $185M private placement

SoftBank-backed OPay secured a proposed $185 million private placement with Standard Bank, giving it a stake of up to 4.99%, subject to OPay's planned US IPO and regulatory approvals. The two companies also plan to explore merchant acquiring, digital banking and other financial services across Africa. Note: TWIF Africa (12 Oct) describes the same commitment as up to $200 million.

Source: Future of Banking, 13 Oct 2026; cross-reference: This Week in Fintech: Africa, 12 Oct 2026

FIS launches Agentic Insights Suite for the office of the CFO

The agentic AI suite lets finance teams manage collections, liquidity, risk and cash flow from a single view. A new forecasting module projects cash across multiple time horizons.

Source: Future of Banking, 13 Oct 2026

UK drawing up mandatory safety reporting for frontier AI companies

Sifted reports the UK government is drawing up plans for mandatory safety reporting by frontier AI companies, as it seeks greater oversight of increasingly powerful models. The same edition notes German climate tech company 1Komma5 raised 70m euros while keeping its IPO on ice.

Source: Sifted Daily, 13 Oct 2026

Also today: N26, KakaoBank, Argentina, BioCatch

N26 appointed former Postbank CFO Marc Hess as CFO, subject to regulatory approval, while remaining under heightened BaFin scrutiny. KakaoBank opened its app to eligible foreign residents in South Korea, with remittances to around 200 countries in over 50 currencies. Argentina's central bank rejected near-term crypto banking integration and kept its 2022 ban. BioCatch says institutions using it through the Alkami Digital Banking Platform stopped over $263M in fraud in 2025.

Source: Future of Banking, 13 Oct 2026

Revolut Watch

Revolut favours a US primary listing if it goes public

CEO Nik Storonsky said that if Revolut lists, it would prefer a primary US listing, a shift from earlier talk of a dual New York and London listing. Reports suggest an IPO in 2028 at the earliest. The newsletter says Revolut is valued at around $115 billion privately and plans credit cards and loans in the US once it gets a full banking charter; it received preliminary approval for a US national banking licence in September.

Source: Future of Banking, 9 Oct and 12 Oct 2026; This Week in Fintech: Exits & Deep Reads, 11 Oct 2026

Revolut to cover costs for 680 customers hit by a data incident

Revolut was tricked into sharing customer files in response to what looked like a request from an Italian government agency, whose domain had been compromised. The incident involved 680 customers worldwide (55 in France) and data that may include ID copies, selfies, IBANs and transaction histories. Revolut says its own systems were not compromised and no funds, access codes or biometric data were affected. It will pay to replace identity documents and will not pay a ransom.

Source: Future of Banking, 8 Oct 2026

Expansion signals: Italy, Singapore, Israel, Philippines

Revolut passed 6 million customers in Italy, ranking third by customer count behind UniCredit and Intesa Sanpaolo; the ECB has ordered it to slow new product launches. It plans to invest $274M in Singapore over five years (TWIF Asia cites $270M), with a permanent office in early 2027. It is ramping up Israeli hiring and moved toward a Philippine digital banking licence. In Hungary, some card payments were unexpectedly refunded, which appears to be a processing error rather than a breach.

Source: Future of Banking, 7, 9 and 12 Oct 2026; This Week in Fintech, 9 and 11 Oct 2026

Payments & Embedded Finance

Banks and fintechs keep adding phone products

Wise added customisable travel eSIM plans in the UK, EEA, US, Canada, Brazil, Australia, New Zealand, Singapore and Malaysia. Starling, Santander, Monzo, N26 and Revolut already offer eSIMs, and Monzo, Revolut and Klarna offer UK phone contracts. Monzo Mobile starts at 8 pounds a month for 10GB of UK data and gets 5% cheaper each year, up to 30% off over time. TWIF cites South Africa, where Capitec and FNB each have over a million customers on SIM plans.

Source: This Week in Fintech: UK and Europe, 8 Oct 2026; Future of Banking, 8 Oct 2026

Anchorage Digital buys Routable and widens USD access

Anchorage Digital acquired Routable, an enterprise payouts platform serving businesses across more than 220 countries. Separately, Anchorage partnered with Standard Chartered so eligible non-US clients can access USD accounts and SWIFT transfers through Anchorage Digital Singapore, with 24/7 USD settlement for counterparties banking with Standard Chartered Singapore.

Source: This Week in Fintech: Exits & Deep Reads, 11 Oct 2026; Future of Banking, 7 and 8 Oct 2026

Wise Platform signs OakNorth; European wallets link up

OakNorth will build Wise's international payments engine into its SME banking platform, supporting Wise Platform's target of 10% of group cross-border volumes. In Europe, Bancomat, Bizum, EPI, SIBS MB WAY and Vipps MobilePay formed the European Network for Payments to link their wallets across over 130 million users in 13 countries.

Source: Future of Banking, 7 Oct 2026; This Week in Fintech: UK and Europe, 8 Oct 2026

Pay by Bank and stablecoin settlement pilots

TrueLayer raised $27M from CDP Venture Capital for an account-to-account network it says serves over 30 million users across 22 countries, and extended its SumUp top-up partnership to Ireland, Germany, France, Spain and Italy. Lloyds and Visa completed a seven-day live pilot settling $750,000 of real obligations in USDC, with funds reaching Visa in under an hour.

Source: Future of Banking, 8 Oct 2026; This Week in Fintech: UK and Europe, 8 Oct 2026

Banking-as-a-Service, Capital & Lending

Coastal Financial hit by class action over its fintech partnership segment

A Pennsylvania retirement fund sued Coastal Financial Corporation, alleging it misled investors about its fintech partnership segment and falsely claimed to be nearly fully indemnified against risk. Credit deterioration in a single $500 million fintech loan portfolio forced a $68.8 million credit expense and a $42.1 million quarterly loss, erasing $470 million in market cap. Coastal is also Pliant's US sponsor bank. A relevant read on sponsor-bank risk.

Source: This Week in Fintech, 9 Oct 2026; Future of Banking, 9 Oct 2026

Plaid's Fall 2026 release puts AI models into credit and fraud

Plaid launched LendScore Arc, its first transformer-based credit risk model. In early testing, Future of Banking reports it increased approvals by 20% for subprime borrowers. TWIF says LendScore 2 predicts repayment 42% better than traditional credit data alone and a new fraud foundation model delivered up to 40% relative improvement over prior baselines. TWIF notes Plaid is its parent company.

Source: Future of Banking, 7 Oct 2026; This Week in Fintech, 9 Oct 2026

Embedded lending and warehouse facilities

Verifone rolled out Commander Capital, powered by YouLend, offering retailers working capital based on their payment activity. In debt financing, Fundbox secured a $175 million asset-backed warehouse facility from MA Financial Group and EarnIn a $150 million warehouse facility from an unnamed Canadian financial institution. German embedded lender finmid raised about 17 million euros in a Series A extension.

Source: This Week in Fintech, 9 Oct and 10 Oct 2026

BaaS consolidation and bank-core wins

Shares completed its acquisition of banking-as-a-service provider Treezor from Societe Generale. Engine by Starling powered Tangerine Bank's new business offering, its third client deployment in a new market within 12 months after Salt Bank in Romania and AMP Bank GO in Australia.

Source: This Week in Fintech: Exits & Deep Reads, 11 Oct 2026; Future of Banking, 7 Oct 2026

Regulatory & Policy

Rain applies for a US national trust bank charter

Stablecoin payments company Rain applied for a charter that would let it custody digital assets and dollars, manage stablecoin reserves and issue dollar-backed stablecoins. It would not take deposits, offer checking accounts or make loans. The filing came three days after the Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC over its trust charter framework. Agora separately received conditional OCC approval for a US national trust bank.

Source: Future of Banking, 7 Oct 2026

UK moves on digital gilts and crypto rules

The UK Treasury named Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Morgan Stanley, NatWest and RBC Capital Markets as joint lead managers for DIGIT, its first digital gilt, to be issued by the first quarter of 2027 and settled onchain within the Digital Securities Sandbox. The FCA opened applications for the UK's new cryptoasset regime.

Source: Future of Banking, 8 Oct 2026; This Week in Fintech: UK and Europe, 8 Oct 2026

Fintech scale meets bank-style scrutiny

Wise is negotiating a bulk settlement with HMRC after a third-party software error gave about 4,000 UK users of its Wise Asset service incorrect tax statements; Wise has fixed the issue and committed to compensate affected users. TWIF notes Wise has 19 million active customers and has processed $243 billion in cross-border payments this year, and argues fintechs at scale will draw bank-level fines.

Source: This Week in Fintech, 9 Oct 2026

Singapore and India tighten the rules

MAS mandated new AI risk rules for financial firms, with compliance required within one year. RBI raised the minimum daily Cash Reserve Ratio maintenance to 99% from 90% effective 16 October, draining about 4 lakh crore rupees of surplus liquidity, and enabled interoperability among Account Aggregators.

Source: This Week in Fintech: Asia rundown, 11 Oct 2026
